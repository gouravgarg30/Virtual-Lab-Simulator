How it works

VirtualLab is a small physics lab that runs in your browser. We made it as our OJT project in 2026 (Harshi Yadav and Gourav Garg).

Assumptions and limits

This is a teaching demo, not a certified engineering tool. It uses simplified ideal-physics models:

  • Battery is ideal: no internal resistance. Wires have zero resistance.
  • Resistors are ideal: the value never changes with heat. Only DC circuits, only series or simple parallel (no capacitors, inductors or AC).
  • Projectile: launched from the ground, lands on flat ground, no air resistance, constant gravity.
  • Pendulum: the formula T = 2π√(L/g) is for small angles. The simulation uses the full pendulum equation, so big angles (like 60°) show a clear difference from the formula.
  • "Matched" means the simulated value is within 2% of the formula. "Needs repeat" means it is outside 2% or the circuit was incomplete.
  • Student / Teacher view is only a switch to show or hide screens. There is no login, so it is not security.
  • Your data stays in this browser (localStorage). Clearing browser data removes it. Use "Reload sample data" in Teacher Review to get the samples back.

How to use each lab

Circuit Lab
  1. Drag a Battery, Resistor and Bulb from the tray onto the lab table (or tap them to add).
  2. Drag from one terminal dot to another to draw a wire. Click a wire and press Delete to remove it.
  3. Click a part to select it. Press R to rotate, Delete to remove, and type new values in Properties.
  4. Press Start simulation. The bulb glows, and the ammeter and voltmeter show real readings. Click a switch to open or close it.
  5. Try the Series and Parallel presets, then type a name and press Save result.

Open circuit: no current flows. Short circuit: no resistance in the path, so you get a warning instead of a misleading huge current.

Projectile Motion

Set speed, angle and gravity, then press Run. Range = v² sin(2θ) / g, time of flight = 2 v sin(θ) / g, maximum height = (v sin θ)² / (2g). The tool compares the step-by-step simulation with these formulas.

Simple Pendulum

Set length, start angle and gravity, then press Run. Theory period T = 2π√(L/g). Try 10° and then 60° to see where the small-angle formula stops being accurate.

Results and Teacher view

Open Results to search, filter, sort and open a row. Switch to Teacher view (top right) to see Teacher Review and to export a CSV of the rows on screen.
